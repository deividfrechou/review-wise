You Can Picture the Finished Piece. That's Never Been the Problem.

You know the deck you want to build out back. The dining table your family would actually gather around. The workbench that finally gets the garage under control.

What gets in the way isn't skill. It's the plan. You find one that looks professional — clean layout, decent photos — buy the lumber, clear your Saturday, and get started. Then around step five, something doesn't line up. A measurement that's close but not quite right. A step that jumps from "cut the side panels" to "attach the face frame" with nothing in between.

A plan that looks complete and a plan that actually works are two very different things.

Most plan collections are built by people who can draw a nice diagram, not by people who've actually stood at a table saw and followed their own instructions from start to finish.

Every Plan Is Built Before It's Published

This library exists because one plan, decades ago, sent a workshop class off the rails — measurements that didn't match real lumber dimensions, and a student who'd already cut his side panels wrong because of it.

Since then, the process has stayed the same: draft it, build it, test every joint, verify every measurement — and if a step is confusing, rewrite it before anyone else sees it. That's grown into a team that builds and tests roughly five new plans a week, every week, for over two decades.

The mistakes get caught in their workshop. Not in yours.
